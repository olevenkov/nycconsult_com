How to structure a practical cloud FinOps review

A useful cloud cost review connects billing data with workloads, owners, and operational decisions. It should produce a prioritized set of actions—not just a list of possible discounts.

1. Establish a reliable baseline

Choose a representative time period and understand major services, accounts, environments, and changes in demand. Separate one-time migration or launch costs from recurring usage where possible.

2. Check allocation and ownership

  • Measure which costs can be assigned to teams, products, or environments.
  • Identify gaps in tags, account structure, and shared-service allocation.
  • Assign owners for material spend and for investigating anomalies.

3. Investigate usage before choosing a fix

Look at utilization, scaling behavior, storage lifecycle, data transfer, idle resources, and service configuration. Confirm workload needs and reliability requirements before recommending a change.

4. Rank actions by value and risk

For each opportunity, record the expected cost effect, implementation effort, owner, validation method, and possible impact on performance, reliability, or security. Prefer reversible, measurable changes where uncertainty is high.

5. Make the process continuous

Set a regular review cadence, budgets or alerts that have owners, and a path to feed cost findings into architecture and provisioning decisions. Cloud FinOps works best as shared practice across engineering, finance, and product teams.
