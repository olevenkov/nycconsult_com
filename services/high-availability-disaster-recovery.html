Resilience engineering

Plan for service disruption before it happens

NYCConsult helps teams set realistic recovery objectives and design high-availability and disaster-recovery approaches that fit service criticality, technical constraints, and operating cost.

Start with business recovery requirements

Different systems have different recovery needs. Agree on service priorities, dependencies, acceptable data loss, and recovery time before selecting redundancy or failover patterns.

  • Define recovery time objectives (RTO) and recovery point objectives (RPO).
  • Map application, identity, network, data, and third-party dependencies.
  • Review backup coverage, restore processes, and recovery ownership.
  • Identify availability risks and single points of failure.

Architecture, runbooks, and exercises

Work can include high-availability architecture options, backup and restore design, failover planning, recovery runbooks, and tabletop or technical exercises. Recovery procedures should be validated and updated as systems change.

Balance resilience and cost

Multi-zone or multi-region designs can reduce some risks but also add operational complexity and cost. Recommendations should reflect the recovery objectives rather than assume that every workload needs the same level of redundancy.
